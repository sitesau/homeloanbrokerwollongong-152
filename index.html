Key Questions Before Lodging an Application

Before selecting a mortgage broker in Wollongong, borrowers should ask questions that encourage clear, straightforward advice. A competent broker should be able to articulate precisely why a recommended loan is suitable for the borrower, what alternative options were considered, what documentation is still outstanding, and what factors might potentially delay the loan approval process.

Consider asking:

  • Which specific lenders are being compared, and does the comparison include both bank and non-bank lending institutions?
  • How is the broker compensated for their services, and are there any direct fees applicable to this specific loan scenario?
  • What information regarding income, expenses, deposit amount, and property details will be used to assess lender serviceability?
  • For a refinance application, is the primary objective a rate review, debt consolidation, equity release, or achieving a simpler loan structure?
  • For construction loans, what evidence is required from the borrower before progress-payment lending can commence?
  • In the case of a self-employed application, what additional document checks should be completed early in the process?

The answers provided should be specific enough for the borrower to understand the immediate next steps and the rationale behind them.

Broker vs. Bank Direct

Going directly to a bank might be suitable for borrowers who have already identified a specific lender and have a straightforward loan application. Engaging a mortgage broker becomes particularly advantageous when a borrower desires a wider choice of lenders, requires assistance in matching lender policies to their unique circumstances, or prefers having a single point of contact to manage the process from pre-approval through to settlement.

It is important to note that Australian mortgage brokers are legally obligated to act in the borrower's best interests and must clearly explain the suitability of recommended loan products. Enquiries are typically handled by brokers accredited with industry bodies such as the MFAA (Mortgage & Finance Association of Australia) or FBAA (Finance Brokers Association of Australia), operating under an Australian Credit Licence. Their credentials and any potential conflicts of interest should be disclosed in writing before the borrower commits to their services. These disclosures help differentiate genuine comparison work from sales-driven pitches.

When making financial decisions, avoid relying on unsubstantiated assumptions about lending thresholds, government grants, or specific lender policies. If a particular figure is significant, such as a deposit requirement, a concession amount, a fee, or an approval timeframe, ask the broker to identify the source of this information and demonstrate how it applies to your exact loan scenario.

  1. Define Your Loan Goal. Clearly state whether your priority is buying a new property, refinancing an existing loan, investing, building a home, arranging bridging finance, or consolidating debt.
  2. Prepare Your Documentation. Gather all necessary documents related to your income, expenses, deposit funds, property details, and business information (if applicable) before seeking lender comparisons.
  3. Compare the Broker's Reasoning. Ask the broker to explain the rationale behind their recommendations, including why a specific loan suits your situation and what alternatives were considered.
  4. Clarify Lender Options. Understand which lenders the broker is comparing and confirm whether both bank and non-bank lenders are included in the assessment.
  5. Understand Broker Remuneration. Inquire about how the broker is paid and if there are any direct fees you will be charged for their services in your specific scenario.

Common questions

What is the main benefit of using a mortgage broker in Wollongong? A mortgage broker can compare loan options from a wide range of banks and non-bank lenders, potentially finding a better fit for your financial situation and property goals than you might find on your own. They also manage the application process, saving you time and effort.

How do mortgage brokers get paid? Typically, mortgage brokers are paid a commission by the lender once the loan settles. Some may also charge a direct fee to the borrower, especially for complex cases. It's important to ask for a clear explanation of their remuneration structure upfront.

Can a mortgage broker help if I'm self-employed? Yes, mortgage brokers often have experience assisting self-employed individuals. They understand the different documentation requirements and lender policies that apply to self-employed applicants and can help you navigate the process.
